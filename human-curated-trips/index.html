How a trip comes together

Two halves of the same trip.

Curators bring what they know — the places, the seasons, the approaches, the warnings. The daily check keeps it current, so what they love reaches you on a week it's worth flying for.

What the curators bring

Everything you only learn by going

Written in their own words, by someone who has been there and wants you to have the good version of the trip.

  • Scout it and say so plainly. Is it actually good, or just famous? Places that don't earn a spot don't get one.
  • Ground-truth the access. Approach times, parking, closures, seasonal bird bans, who owns the land, who to ask.
  • Pin the real season. The weeks it's in, the weeks it's a swamp, and the two shoulder weeks worth gambling on.
  • Solve the logistics. Where to sleep, whether you need a car, how to get there from the airport at 23:40.
  • Plan the bad day. Rest-day options, the wet-weather fallback, the crag that stays dry in a downpour.
  • Write the brief. What to bring, what to skip, and the one thing that will catch you out.

What the daily check adds

Everything that changes while you're at work

Every place on their shortlist, every date in your window, every morning.

  • Conditions, per sport. Snow depth and lift status, swell and period, wind hours, hours of dry rock, rain risk.
  • Flights from your airports. Price, time of day, and whether Friday night still works after the delay.
  • Beds for those dates. Hostel and dorm prices, moving as the dates fill up.
  • What people are saying. Forum, Reddit and local-club chatter from the last 14 days — conditions reports beat forecasts.
  • Crowds and daylight. School holidays, festival weeks, sunset at the parking, tides where they matter.
  • The trade-off. It weighs all of it and returns one answer: go here, on these dates, for this reason.

Our independence

Nobody can buy their way onto the board.

A place is on this list for one reason: someone who loves it put it there and will stand behind it. That's the whole promise, and it's worth saying out loud, because it's the part that makes the rest worth reading.

No paid placement

No sponsored entries, no advertising, no affiliate deal quietly bending a ranking. Destinations and operators cannot pay to appear or to move up.

Curators aren't on anyone's payroll

No comped stays, no fees from the places they cover. If that ever changes for an entry, the entry says so, in the open, at the top.

We'll tell you when to skip it

Half the value of a curated list is what's been left off it — and the honest warning attached to what stayed on.

Wrong gets fixed

Places change and entries go stale. Tell us and a curator re-checks it; if it no longer deserves the spot, it comes off.

How a place earns a spot

Four gates, in order. Most places stop at two.

Gate 01

Nominated

A curator or a trusted local puts it forward in their own words, with a reason and a season.

Gate 02

Seconded

A second curator who knows the area reads it, and local clubs get a say. Claims nobody will back kill the entry.

Gate 03

Logistics solved

Beds, transfers and an approach that works without a hire car — or it's marked car-only, honestly.

Gate 04

On the board

It goes live and we start watching the conditions daily. Re-read by its curator each season.
